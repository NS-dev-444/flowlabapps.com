PaperCrest

Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 18, 2026 · Applies to PaperCrest for iOS, iPadOS, Android, all versions

The short version. PaperCrest collects nothing and cannot send anything. The iOS app contains no networking code and the Android app has no network permission, so your scans, their text and everything the app works out about them stay on your device.

What we collect What is stored Permissions Network Third parties Store declarations Children Your rights Contact

This Privacy Policy explains how Flowlab Apps (“we”, “us”) handles information in PaperCrest (the “app”). It is written to be read, not to be survived.

1. What we collect

Nothing. There is no sign-up, no login, no email address collected, no advertising identifier, no usage analytics and no crash reporting. No SDK in the app reports your behaviour to anyone.

Flowlab Apps operates no server that holds your PaperCrest data, because no such data ever reaches us. That is not a policy decision we could quietly reverse in an update — there is no backend to send it to.

2. What the app stores, and where

Everything PaperCrest saves is stored on the device you are using it on.

WhatWhereWhy
Scans and imported pages, as captured and as processedIn the app's private storage on your deviceSo you can view, search, export and share them
Recognised text, document type, title and extracted details, such as a receipt's total or a business card's contact fieldsIn the app's database on your deviceTo title, file and search your documents. A protected document keeps these inside its encrypted payload instead
Where sensitive values were found on a page: a card number, a date of birthIn the app's database, as positions and kinds, never the values themselvesTo warn you and to offer them for redaction
Protected documentsEncrypted on your device with AES-256-GCM. The key is held in the iOS Keychain or the Android Keystore behind your face, fingerprint or passcode and never leaves the deviceSo a document you protect cannot be read without you, even from a backup
Folders, tags, favourites and the TrashIn the app's database on your deviceTo organise your library. Tags are not encrypted, even on a protected document
Copies made for sharingA temporary folder the app empties at its next launch and, on iPhone and iPad, excludes from backupSo the share sheet has a file to hand over. On iPhone and iPad, Settings › Clear temporary files removes them sooner
Settings, such as whether App Lock is onOn your deviceTo remember how you like the app to work

3. Permissions

PaperCrest asks for the following, and nothing else. Every permission marked optional can be declined, and the app keeps working without it.

  • Camera — To scan. Asked for from the first-run screen, after the app has explained why, and you can import instead of granting it.
  • Face ID, Touch ID or fingerprint (optional) — Only when you turn on App Lock or protect a document. The check happens in the operating system; the app never receives biometric data. On Android the biometric library declares the ordinary USE_BIOMETRIC and USE_FINGERPRINT permissions, which need no runtime grant.
  • Photos and files — Imported through the system picker, which runs outside the app and hands over only the items you choose, so no photo-library permission is requested. Sending a business card to Contacts works the same way, through the share sheet: the app asks for no Contacts access.

4. Network activity

None, and this is enforced rather than promised. On iPhone and iPad the app links no networking framework and contains no networking code, and an audit script checks the built binary for both before every upload. On Android the app declares no INTERNET permission, so the operating system refuses it a network socket; the build fails if that permission ever appears, and an on-device test tries to open a connection and requires it to fail. There is no account, no sync and no server. Everything works in Airplane Mode. The Privacy Policy link in Settings opens this page in your browser. The browser makes that request, not PaperCrest.

5. Third parties

None receive anything. The iOS app ships with zero third-party libraries. The Android app uses Google's AndroidX, CameraX and ML Kit text recognition with its model bundled in the app; ML Kit's telemetry components are removed from the manifest and, with no network permission, would have nothing to send through anyway. There are no analytics libraries, crash reporters or advertising SDKs.

We do not sell, rent, share or disclose your personal information, because we do not hold any of it.

6. What we declare to the app stores

Apple and Google each require a privacy declaration alongside the app listing. These are the answers we give for PaperCrest, reproduced here so you can check them against the rest of this policy.

Apple App Store — App Privacy Data Not Collected. Nothing is collected from this app, by us or by anyone else, so no data type is linked to you and none is used to track you.
Google Play — Data safety No data collected. No data shared with third parties. No data is transmitted off the device, so there is none to encrypt in transit. Users can erase everything the app holds by deleting it.
Account deletion Not applicable — the app has no account. See Data & Account Deletion.

On iPhone and iPad, unprotected documents and their text are part of your normal device backup (iCloud Backup or a computer backup) and come back on a new phone. Protected documents do not: their key never leaves the device, so remove protection before you move phones if you want to keep them. On Android, nothing in the app is backed up or transferred by the system. Any backup is made by Apple under your own device settings; we never receive it, which is why the declarations above still read no data collected.

7. Children

PaperCrest is not directed at children under 13, and it collects no personal information from anyone of any age. Because nothing is collected, nothing is collected from children.

8. Additional notes

Recognised text, document types, titles and extracted fields are computed on the device, by Apple's Vision framework on iPhone and iPad, and by Google's on-device ML Kit model on Android, and never leave it. Sensitive-data detection is a heuristic: the app stores where a value is and what kind it is, never the value, and it never claims a document is free of sensitive information, only that nothing was detected. A document leaves the app only when you share or export it. Exported PDFs carry the document's title and the creator name “PaperCrest” and nothing else: no author, no location, no device identifier. Exported images carry no metadata at all, not the camera's, and not the location an imported photo came with. Deleting a document moves it to the Trash; Empty Trash removes the app's copies of its files and rewrites the database so nothing lingers. If you lose the face, fingerprint or passcode that guards a protected document, it cannot be recovered; there is no escrow, by design.

9. Your rights and your choices

Privacy laws including the GDPR and the CCPA give you rights to access, correct, export and delete personal data a company holds about you. Flowlab Apps holds no personal data about PaperCrest users, so there is nothing for us to produce, correct or erase on request.

You remain in full control of the data on your own device:

  • Delete individual items from inside the app.
  • Remove the app to delete its local data, subject to your device's own backup and restore settings.
  • Revoke any permission at any time from your device's system settings.

We do not sell personal information and never have. We do not share personal information for cross-context behavioural advertising.

10. Security

Because your data stays on your device, its security rests on your device's own protections — your passcode, your biometric lock and your operating system's storage encryption. Keep your device updated and locked.

11. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the revised version will be published at this address with a new date at the top. If a future version of PaperCrest ever begins collecting data, this policy will be updated before that version is released, and the app will ask for your consent in-app.
